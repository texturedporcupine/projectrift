# SF Bay Area Shared Postcard Direct Mail — Business Plan

## Executive Summary

Bay Area Direct Mail Co. operates a shared postcard direct mail advertising business serving affluent suburban communities in the San Francisco Bay Area. The business acts as an advertising broker and project manager: selling exclusive ad spaces to local service businesses, coordinating professional postcard design and printing, and handling USPS Every Door Direct Mail (EDDM) delivery to 5,000–8,000 households per campaign.

The Bay Area market commands 20–40% higher pricing than comparable Texas markets due to higher business revenue, cost-of-living, and homeowner spending power. Target first-year revenue: $25,000–60,000 across 6–12 campaigns.

## 1. Legal & Regulatory Setup (California)

### 1.1 Business Structure

**Recommended starting structure: Sole Proprietorship with DBA**

A sole proprietorship is the simplest and fastest way to begin operations in California. No state filing required for the entity itself — just a DBA (Fictitious Business Name) filing and local business license.

**If upgrading to LLC:**
- File Articles of Organization with the California Secretary of State ($70 filing fee)
- **Critical:** California imposes an **$800/year minimum franchise tax** regardless of revenue (CA Franchise Tax Board)
- First-year waiver may apply if the LLC is formed in Q4 (check current FTB rules)
- LLC provides liability protection but adds cost and annual filing requirements
- Recommendation: start as sole prop, convert to LLC once revenue exceeds $30,000/year

### 1.2 Fictitious Business Name (FBN / DBA)

California requires a Fictitious Business Name statement for any business operating under a name other than the owner's legal name.

**Filing process:**
1. Search the county recorder's website to verify name availability
2. File FBN statement with the **county recorder** (not the county clerk — this is different from Texas)
3. Cost: $26–$100 depending on county (Contra Costa: ~$40, Santa Clara: ~$52, Alameda: ~$47)
4. Within 30 days of filing, **publish the FBN in an adjudicated local newspaper** for 4 consecutive weeks
5. Publication cost: $50–$150 depending on newspaper
6. After publication, file the **affidavit of publication** with the county recorder
7. Keep the affidavit on file — the FBN expires after **5 years** and must be renewed

**Recommended adjudicated newspapers by county:**
- Contra Costa: Contra Costa Times, Danville Weekly, San Ramon Valley Times
- Alameda: The Independent (Livermore), Pleasanton Weekly, Daily Review
- Santa Clara: Los Gatos Weekly-Times, Saratoga News, Palo Alto Weekly

### 1.3 Business License

California does not have a statewide business license. However, **most California cities and counties require a local business license** (also called a business tax certificate).

| County / City | Approximate Cost | Where to Apply |
|--------------|-----------------|----------------|
| Danville (Contra Costa) | $50–150/year | Town of Danville Finance Dept |
| San Ramon | $50–100/year | City of San Ramon Business License |

| Dublin | $50–100/year | City of Dublin Business License |

Check with your city's business license office. Some cities base the fee on gross receipts.

### 1.4 Sales Tax Considerations

California's treatment of advertising and printing services:

- **Advertising services** (strategy, design, media buying) are **generally not taxable** in California
- **Printed materials sold to clients** may be taxable depending on how the transaction is structured
- If you are selling a tangible product (the printed postcards) to the advertiser, sales tax may apply
- If you are selling an advertising service that includes printing as a component, it's generally exempt
- **Recommendation:** Structure invoices as "advertising services" rather than "printed materials"
- Consult the **CA CDTFA** (California Department of Tax and Fee Administration) at cdtfa.ca.gov for definitive guidance
- If required, register for a seller's permit with CDTFA (no cost)

### 1.5 Insurance

- **General liability insurance:** $500–1,000/year for a small advertising business
- Covers: property damage, bodily injury, advertising injury claims
- Recommended carrier: look at Hiscox, Next Insurance, or a local broker
- Not legally required for a sole prop, but strongly recommended

## 4. Sales Process

### 4.1 Prospecting Pipeline

1. **Research** — Build 30–40 business prospect list per community across 8–10 categories
2. **Rate prospects** — IDEAL, GOOD, or STRONG based on fit, reviews, location, accessibility
3. **3-email sequence** — Cold opener (Day 1), follow-up (Day 3), value-add close (Day 7)
4. **Phone follow-up** — Call IDEAL-rated prospects after email sequence
5. **In-person meeting** — For premium placement prospects or $800+ ad spaces
6. **Close** — Service agreement + 50% deposit

## 5. Operations Workflow

### 5.1 Campaign Lifecycle (6–8 weeks)

| Week | Activity |
|------|----------|
| 1–2 | Prospect research, outreach, email sequences |
| 2–3 | Phone follow-ups, meetings, closing |
| 3–4 | Collect ad content, begin design |
| 4–5 | Proof approval cycle (3 business days per round) |
| 5 | Collect final payments, prepare print files |
| 5–6 | Print production (3–5 business day turnaround) |
| 6 | Bundle, EDDM paperwork, DDU drop-off |
| 8 | Advertiser follow-up, testimonials, renewal pitch |

### 5.2 EDDM Process

1. Go to eddm.usps.com → create a new mailing
2. Enter target zip code → select carrier routes
3. Filter for demographics (HHI, household size)
4. Generate PS Form 3587
5. Print facing slips for each carrier route bundle
6. Bundle postcards by route (face up, address side visible)
7. Drive to DDU (Destination Delivery Unit) post office
8. Pay $0.247/piece at drop-off
9. Get receipt as delivery confirmation
10. Expected in-home delivery: 3–5 business days after drop-off

### 5.3 Overlapping Campaigns

Once established, run overlapping campaigns for efficiency:
- While Campaign A is in design/print, start prospecting for Campaign B
- While Campaign B is in outreach, deliver Campaign A
- Target: 2 campaigns running concurrently by month 4

## 6. Growth Strategy

### 6.1 Phase 1: Proof of Concept (Months 1–3)
- Launch 2–3 campaigns in Danville / San Ramon / Pleasanton
- Establish processes and templates
- Collect testimonials and case studies
- Target: $6,000–10,000 revenue

### 6.2 Phase 2: Expansion (Months 4–6)
- Add Dublin, Los Gatos, Livermore to rotation
- Implement multi-campaign discounts for retention
- Begin overlapping campaign scheduling
- Target: $12,000–20,000 cumulative revenue

### 6.3 Phase 3: Scale (Months 7–12)
- 8–12 communities in rotation
- 50%+ advertiser renewal rate
- Hire part-time help for design or delivery
- Explore adjacent markets (Walnut Creek, Lafayette, Moraga)
- Target: $25,000–60,000 annual revenue

### 6.4 Long-Term Opportunities
- **Digital companion:** Offer QR code tracking on postcards linking to landing pages
- **Community sponsorships:** Partner with HOAs for co-branded campaigns
- **Vertical expansion:** Industry-specific postcards (e.g., all home services, all medical)
- **Geographic expansion:** Sacramento metro (Rocklin, Roseville, Folsom)
