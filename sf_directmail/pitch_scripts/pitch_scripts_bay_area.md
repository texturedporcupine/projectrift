# Bay Area Shared Postcard Direct Mail — Category Pitch Scripts

*10 category-specific scripts. Use on phone calls and in-person visits.*
*Each script: Hook → What It Is → Exclusivity Pitch → ROI Math → Objection Handling → Close.*
*Swap [COMMUNITY], [HH COUNT], [PRICE], and [HOOK DETAIL] for your specific campaign.*

---

## HOW TO USE THESE SCRIPTS

These are conversation guides, not teleprompters. Know the structure; don't read word-for-word.
- **Hook** (10 seconds): earn the next 60 seconds
- **What it is** (30 seconds): simple, no jargon
- **Exclusivity** (15 seconds): the key differentiator
- **ROI math** (30 seconds): one customer covers the cost
- **Objection handling**: see section at the end of each script
- **Close**: always ask for the deposit, not "more time to think"

## UNIVERSAL OBJECTION HANDLER

*(Use for any category when you hit an objection not covered above.)*

### "We don't do direct mail."
"Most businesses I call say the same thing — and usually it's because they tried a solo piece or a coupon book where 30 competitors were on the same page. This is different: you're the only [CATEGORY] on this postcard. The exclusivity completely changes the math."

### "We tried it before and it didn't work."
"What format? [Let them answer.] That's the distinction. When you're sharing a coupon pack with competitors, there's no reason for a homeowner to choose you. When you're the only option in your category on a professional, well-designed card, you're the clear choice. You're not competing on the page."

### "Too expensive."
"$[PRICE] sounds like a lot until you do the math. You're reaching [HH COUNT] households for $[PRICE/HH COUNT] cents per home. A Google Ads click in [CATEGORY] costs $10-40. One new customer who stays for a year covers your entire investment. The question isn't whether it's expensive — it's whether one new customer is worth more than $[PRICE]."

### "Need to think about it."
"Totally fair — what's the specific concern? [Let them answer. Address it directly.] Here's the thing: once I fill this slot, the exclusivity is gone. I'm not trying to pressure you — I just want to give you a real chance at it before I move to the next business on my list. What would it take to make a decision today?"

### "Send me more information."
"Happy to — I'll send you the full campaign overview. When you get it, what's the one thing you'll be looking at first to decide if it's worth it?" [Use their answer to follow up on exactly that point.] "Mind if I follow up with you on [SPECIFIC DAY]?"

### "We have a corporate marketing department."
"Understood — does marketing decisions flow through them for local programs, or do you have some autonomy at the local level? If you need their sign-off, I'm happy to send materials that make it easy to present internally. What's the timeline on that?"
