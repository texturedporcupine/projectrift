## 1. Business Overview

### What This Business Is

A direct mail advertising brokerage. We sell exclusive ad space to local businesses on a shared postcard, manage all design and print coordination, and deliver via USPS Every Door Direct Mail (EDDM) to targeted Bay Area neighborhoods. Each advertiser gets one exclusive category — no competing dentists, no competing HVAC companies on the same card.

### Why It Works

- **EDDM economics:** $0.247/piece means reaching 6,000 households costs ~$1,482. Shared among 6-8 advertisers, the postage cost per advertiser is under $250.
- **Exclusivity:** Advertisers aren't competing for attention on the same page. One dentist per card = 100% of the dental consideration from that mailing.
- **No list-buying:** EDDM reaches every household on selected carrier routes. No opt-in required.
- **Bay Area premium pricing:** Business revenue and cost-of-living expectations support 20-40% higher ad rates than comparable Texas or Midwest markets.

### Core Value Proposition

For advertisers: reach every household in a target neighborhood — no filter, no algorithm — for less than the cost of a Facebook ad campaign, with category exclusivity.

For the operator: build a scalable, repeatable revenue model with low startup costs and compounding returns (renewals, referrals, multi-community campaigns).

## 2. Legal Structure & California Compliance

### 2.1 Business Structure

**Recommended starting structure:** Sole proprietorship operating under a Fictitious Business Name (FBN). Simplest to set up, no separate tax return, no minimum franchise tax.

**LLC option:** Provides liability protection but adds complexity:
- $800/year minimum California Franchise Tax Board fee regardless of revenue
- Separate LLC tax return (Form 568)
- First-year franchise tax may be waived if LLC is formed October-December (Q4) — the $800 minimum is not due until the second year

**Recommendation:** Start as sole proprietorship. Form LLC after your second campaign if revenue trajectory justifies it.

### 2.2 Fictitious Business Name (FBN / DBA)

California law (Business and Professions Code § 17900 et seq.) requires anyone doing business under a name other than their legal name to file a Fictitious Business Name statement.

**Filing process:**
1. Go to your **county recorder's office** (not the county clerk — these are separate offices in California)
2. Complete the Fictitious Business Name Statement form
3. Pay the filing fee: $26-$100 depending on county
4. Receive your stamped FBN statement

**Publication requirement:**
- Within 30 days of filing, publish the FBN statement in an **adjudicated newspaper** in the county where the principal place of business is located
- Publication: 4 consecutive weeks
- Cost: $50-150 depending on the newspaper
- After publication, the newspaper provides an **Affidavit of Publication** — file this with the county recorder
- FBN registration is valid for **5 years** and must be renewed

**Adjudicated newspapers by community:**
- Danville / San Ramon (Contra Costa County): Danville Weekly, Pleasanton Weekly, Contra Costa Times
- Pleasanton / Dublin (Alameda County): Pleasanton Weekly, East Bay Times, Tri-Valley Times
- Los Gatos (Santa Clara County): Los Gatos Weekly-Times, San Jose Mercury News (adjudicated section)

### 2.3 Business Licenses

California has **no statewide business license**. However, most cities and counties require a local business license.

### 2.4 Sales Tax

California's sales tax treatment of advertising and printing services:

- **Advertising services (your fees for selling ad space):** Generally **not taxable** in California. You are selling an advertising service, not a physical product.
- **Printed materials:** If you purchase printed postcards and resell them as part of your service, the printing cost may be subject to sales tax. Under California law, "printing and related services" have specific tax treatment that depends on how the transaction is structured.
- **Safe approach:** Structure your contracts so advertisers are paying for an "advertising service" that happens to include printing — rather than paying for physical printed materials. Consult the **California Department of Tax and Fee Administration (CDTFA)** at cdtfa.ca.gov or call 1-800-400-7115 for a formal determination.

### 2.5 Seller's Permit

If you determine that any portion of your revenue is subject to sales tax, you must obtain a **seller's permit** from the CDTFA (free). Register at cdtfa.ca.gov.

### 2.6 Federal Tax

- **Sole proprietorship:** Report business income and expenses on Schedule C (Form 1040). Pay self-employment tax (15.3%) on net profit.
- **Estimated taxes:** If you expect to owe $1,000+ in federal taxes, make quarterly estimated payments (Form 1040-ES). Due: April 15, June 15, September 15, January 15.
- **California state estimated taxes:** Use Form 540-ES. Same quarterly schedule.
- **EIN:** Optional for sole proprietors, but recommended. Apply free at irs.gov/EIN.

### 2.7 Insurance

Not legally required but strongly recommended:
- **General liability insurance:** $300-600/year. Covers claims arising from your advertising services.
- **Professional liability (E&O) insurance:** Optional. Covers claims that your ad caused harm to an advertiser's business.

Direct mail competitors in the Bay Area:
- **ValPak / Money Mailer:** Coupon packs with 20-40 advertisers. Low production quality. No exclusivity. Bay Area advertisers are often skeptical due to past experience.

## 6. EDDM Workflow

### Step 1: Route Selection (eddm.usps.com)

1. Go to eddm.usps.com → "EDDM Retail" (for quantities under 5,000 at a single post office) or "EDDM BMEU" (for larger quantities, better for 5,000+)
2. Enter your target zip code
3. Use the mapping tool to select carrier routes
4. Filter by: household type (residential), income if available, household size
5. Note the exact household count for each route (you'll need this for the manifest)
6. Lock your routes by saving/downloading the route list

### Step 2: Design Requirements

- Postcard must qualify as a USPS flat mail piece:
  - Minimum dimensions: >6.125" tall OR >10.5" long
  - Maximum dimensions: 12" × 15"
  - Minimum thickness: 0.007" (14pt cardstock)
- EDDM indicia must appear in the upper-right corner of the address side
- Leave the bottom 3.5" of the address side clear for USPS use
- "Local Postal Customer" or "Residential Customer" as the addressee (no individual names)

### Step 3: Print Requirements

- Order quantity = exact USPS household count + 50-100 extras (for samples/proofs)
- Printer must produce EDDM-compliant postcards
- Request a physical proof before the full run

### Step 4: Bundling (EDDM Retail)

1. Sort postcards into bundles of 50-200 pieces
2. Place a facing slip on top of each bundle (download from eddm.usps.com)
3. Rubber band each bundle
4. Complete PS Form 3587 (EDDM Retail Facing Slips and Postage Statement)

### Step 5: DDU Drop-Off

1. Call the DDU (Destination Delivery Unit) that serves your target zip code to confirm drop-off procedure and hours
2. Bring: bundled postcards, PS Form 3587, payment (check, money order, or Business Reply Mail account)
3. Pay $0.247 per piece at drop-off
4. Get a receipt — this is your delivery confirmation

### Step 6: Delivery Timeline

EDDM typically delivers within 2-7 business days of DDU drop-off, depending on mail volume and local carrier schedule.
