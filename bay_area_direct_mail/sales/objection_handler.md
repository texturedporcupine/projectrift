# Universal objection handler

> Carried over from the Austin playbook **largely unchanged** — language is
> market-neutral. The only Bay Area edits are the example numbers and zip
> references woven into a few of the responses.

Use this any time a prospect raises an objection during cold call, follow-up
call, in-person meeting, or post-proposal. Each section follows the same
shape: **acknowledge -> reframe -> evidence -> easy ask**.

---

## "We don't do direct mail."

**Acknowledge.** "I get that — most {{category}} owners say the same thing
when I open the conversation."

**Reframe.** "This isn't a solo postcard or a coupon mailer. It's a single,
high-quality shared piece going to one zip — with **one company per category**.
You're not competing with three other {{category}} ads in the same week;
you're the only one."

**Evidence.** "On a $0.247 postage rate plus ~$0.06 in print, you're at
about **$0.30 per household impression** in {{community}}. Per impression
that's cheaper than basically anything else you can buy in this zip."

**Easy ask.** "Worth a 10-minute look at the one-pager? Then you can tell me
no on real numbers, not on the category."

---

## "We tried mail before and it didn't work."

**Acknowledge.** "That's the most common reason {{category}} owners pass.
Almost always it was a solo postcard with three or four competitors' ads
hitting the same week."

**Reframe.** "**One business per category** is the entire product here. No
competing dentists, no competing HVAC companies, nobody else in your
category on the piece. That's a different mechanism than what you tried."

**Evidence.** "On the typical {{community}} drop, even at a conservative
0.5% response, that's ~30 inquiries on a single $0.247/piece campaign — and
**one closed {{category}} job typically pays for the slot**. You don't need
the campaign to be a flood; you need it to be a quiet, predictable trickle."

**Easy ask.** "Want me to send over a one-pager with the math?"

---

## "Too expensive."

**Acknowledge.** "Fair — it's the second most common pushback I hear, and
it's worth pulling apart."

**Reframe.** "Compare it to your other channels: Google Ads in {{community}}
for {{category}} runs $80-$180 per click, and a {{category}} lead magnet
costs $40-$120 in the local market. The shared postcard is roughly **$0.10
per impression** to ~6,000 {{community}} households. Per impression, it's
the cheapest channel you can buy at this geography level."

**Evidence.** "Break-even on the slot is **one closed job**. On {{category}},
that's basically free at average response rates."

**Easy ask.** "If I could show you a tier-2 community at a lower price
point — would that fit better, or is the issue the channel rather than
the spend?"

---

## "I need to think about it."

**Acknowledge.** "Of course — for any decision in the $500-$1,000 range
that's the right instinct."

**Reframe.** "The only thing pulling on the timeline is the category lock —
once I close the {{category}} slot to {{competitor_name}} (or anyone else
in {{community}}), you can't have it on this campaign. So the question
isn't 'will I do this' — it's 'do I want me to **hold** the slot for
{{hold_window}} or **release** it'?"

**Evidence.** "I won't sell you twice; I won't follow up daily. One yes-or-no
in {{hold_window}} and we either move forward or I close the slot."

**Easy ask.** "Hold or release? I'm happy with either."

---

## "How do I track ROI?"

**Acknowledge.** "Smart question — and the right one to ask."

**Reframe.** "You have three real options, in increasing order of fidelity:"

1. **Dedicated phone number on the card** (CallRail, simple, $30-50/mo).
   Every call from the campaign rings through a unique number that
   forwards to your line. You see exact attribution per call.
2. **Unique URL or QR code** to a tracking landing page (free if you have
   a website, $20/mo otherwise).
3. **'How did you hear about us'** form field added to your intake. Costs
   nothing, less precise than the first two, but still useful.

**Evidence.** "Most of my advertisers do option 1 — a tracking phone
number costs ~$30/mo and tells you exactly how many calls came from the
campaign over the 90-day life of the postcard."

**Easy ask.** "Want me to include a setup recommendation in your one-pager?"

---

## "We don't have time to design an ad right now."

**Acknowledge.** "Totally fair — most owners don't have time for design work
on top of running the business."

**Reframe.** "Design is included in the slot price. You give me a logo, a
phone number, a URL, and one offer. **I do the rest** and send you a proof.
You approve the proof, I print and drop. Total time from your side is
usually 20-30 minutes."

**Evidence.** "I'll typically have a first proof to you within 5 business
days of signing. Two rounds of revisions are included if you want to tweak
copy or layout."

**Easy ask.** "Want me to send over the brief I'd use to build the proof?
It's a single page."

---

## "Can I see proofs from past campaigns?"

**Acknowledge.** "Yes, absolutely — that's the right question to ask before
signing."

**Reframe.** "I'll send a proof gallery with my one-pager. Note that early
proofs are masked for client confidentiality, but you'll see layout, type
hierarchy, and how 6-8 ads can sit on a single piece without looking
crowded."

**Easy ask.** "What's the best email to send the gallery to?"

> *Note for the operator:* until you have proofs of your own, use designer
> mockups (Figma / Adobe stock) of a sample card. Be honest that it's a
> mockup; clarity beats false provenance every time.

---

## "We're already booked solid — we don't need leads."

**Acknowledge.** "Best problem to have — and the answer is to **upgrade
the book**, not add to it."

**Reframe.** "A campaign in {{community}} skews higher-AOV than your
average lead source — bigger lots, higher disposable income, more
willingness to pay for premium service. So saying yes today doesn't
mean adding stress; it means swapping low-margin work for high-margin
work as natural turnover happens."

**Easy ask.** "Want me to send the one-pager and we can talk about
positioning the offer for your highest-margin service line specifically?"

---

## "Who else is on the card?"

**Acknowledge.** "Great question — that's literally the product."

**Reframe.** "I won't share the other advertisers' names until we sign,
but I can tell you the **categories** I'm filling: {{filled_categories}}.
What I can promise is that I will **never put a competing {{category}}
business on the same campaign**. That's in the agreement."

**Evidence.** "If a competing {{category}} business reaches out after you
sign, I close the door. That's the exclusivity."

**Easy ask.** "Want to lock in the {{category}} slot before I open the
next category to outreach?"

---

## "Can I get out of it if it doesn't work?"

**Acknowledge.** "Fair question — and the honest answer is: depends on
where in the timeline."

**Reframe.** "Before proof sign-off, you can cancel and get **75% of your
deposit back** — I just keep enough to cover the time and design work
already done. **After proof sign-off, we're committed** because print and
postage are non-refundable on my side."

**Evidence.** "Almost no one cancels post-proof; the proof round is
specifically designed to make sure everyone's confident before we commit."

**Easy ask.** "Sound fair? If so, I'll send the agreement."

---

## "Send me something I can review."

**Acknowledge.** "Done — I'll send the one-pager today."

**Reframe.** "Two questions while we're on the phone:" *(then ask)*

1. "What email is best?"
2. "When in the next week or so do you want me to follow up — Wednesday or Friday?"

**Why this works:** "Send me something" is usually a soft no. Pinning a
follow-up date in the same call converts most of those soft nos into
actual second calls.
