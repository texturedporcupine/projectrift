# California/Bay Area Business Plan Sections

Use these sections to replace Texas-specific legal, tax, market, and launch
planning content in an Austin-style shared postcard direct mail business plan.

## 1. Business overview

The business sells exclusive category ad space on a shared direct mail postcard
delivered through USPS Every Door Direct Mail (EDDM). Each campaign targets a
defined Bay Area community with clear geographic boundaries and enough
households to support 5,000-8,000 delivered postcards.

The operator acts as the advertising broker and project manager:

1. Select a target community and confirm EDDM carrier routes.
2. Sell 6-8 non-competing ad spaces.
3. Collect a 50% deposit from each advertiser at signing.
4. Coordinate postcard design, proof approval, printing, bundling, and EDDM
   paperwork.
5. Collect the final 50% before print production.
6. Drop bundled mail at the Destination Delivery Unit serving the routes.
7. Follow up with advertisers two weeks after delivery and pitch renewal.

Category exclusivity is the core offer. A postcard can include one HVAC company,
one dentist, one pest control company, one pool service, and so on, but never two
direct competitors in the same category.

## 2. California legal and tax setup

This section is operational guidance, not legal or tax advice. Confirm final
requirements with the relevant California city, county, the CDTFA, and a
qualified professional before launch.

### 2.1 Business structure

A sole proprietorship is the simplest starting structure for a low-overhead
broker/project-manager model. It avoids California's LLC minimum franchise tax
while the concept is being validated.

If liability separation or partnership structure is needed, an LLC may be
appropriate, but California LLCs generally owe an $800 annual minimum franchise
tax regardless of revenue. Budget for that cost before forming one.

### 2.2 Fictitious Business Name

If operating under a name that does not include the owner's legal surname, file a
Fictitious Business Name (FBN) statement with the county recorder where the
business is based. California commonly uses "FBN" rather than Texas-style DBA
language.

Operational checklist:

- Search county FBN records for name availability.
- File the FBN statement with the county recorder.
- Publish the FBN notice in an adjudicated local newspaper for 4 consecutive
  weeks within 30 days of filing.
- Keep the affidavit of publication on file.
- Renew the FBN on the county schedule, commonly every 5 years.

Expected county filing and publication budget: roughly $75-250 combined,
depending on county and newspaper.

### 2.3 Local business license

California does not issue one statewide general business license. Most cities
and some counties require a local business license or business tax certificate.
Check the city where the business is operated and any city where a physical
office is maintained.

Expected annual budget: roughly $50-200 for a small service business, varying by
city.

### 2.4 Sales tax and printed materials

Advertising and marketing services are generally treated differently from the
sale of tangible printed goods. California may tax printed materials or
print-related transfers to clients, even if the project includes non-taxable
service components.

Before invoicing, verify whether the offer is structured as:

- advertising project management only;
- resale of printed postcards;
- a mixed service plus tangible goods transaction; or
- reimbursement for postage and print production.

Use the California Department of Tax and Fee Administration (CDTFA) as the
source of truth, and document whether a seller's permit is required.

### 2.5 Contracts and approvals

Each advertiser should sign a short service agreement covering:

- campaign community and estimated delivery range;
- category exclusivity;
- ad size and placement type;
- payment schedule;
- proof approval deadline;
- no guarantee of lead volume or sales;
- advertiser responsibility for substantiating claims, licenses, and offers;
- California governing law; and
- permission to use campaign results or testimonials if separately approved.

## 3. Bay Area market positioning

The Bay Area version should lean into affluent suburban households, high home
values, and local service providers competing for repeat homeowner demand.

Best-fit communities have:

- 5,000-8,000 mailable households in a compact route cluster;
- median household income above $140K;
- high homeownership;
- HOA, neighborhood association, or community newsletter activity;
- nearby commercial corridors with local businesses that can serve the area; and
- visible spend on home maintenance, family services, health, and luxury
  services.

Recommended first communities:

| Community | Zip codes | Tier | Positioning |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Danville | 94526 | 1 | Affluent homeowners, strong community identity, local-service culture |
| San Ramon | 94582, 94583 | 1-2 | Master-planned neighborhoods, tech families, high homeownership |
| Pleasanton | 94566, 94588 | 2 | Established family suburbs, strong home-service demand |
| Dublin | 94568 | 2 | Growth market, young families, expanding local business base |
| Los Gatos | 95030, 95032 | 1 | Premium home, wellness, and luxury service market |

## 4. Pricing and revenue model

Bay Area pricing should sit 20-40% above an Austin baseline.

| Tier | Example communities | Standard ad | Premium placement |
| --- | --- | ---: | ---: |
| Tier 1 - Premium | Danville, Los Gatos, Saratoga, Los Altos, Palo Alto | $700-900 | $1,100-1,400 |
| Tier 2 - Strong | San Ramon, Pleasanton, Dublin | $550-750 | $850-1,100 |
| Tier 3 - Growth | Fremont, Livermore, Brentwood, Rocklin/Roseville | $400-600 | $650-850 |

First-campaign target:

- 5-6 advertisers at $500-700 each.
- Revenue: $2,500-4,200.
- Printing: approximately $350.
- EDDM postage: 5,000 pieces at $0.247 = $1,235.
- Miscellaneous supplies and travel: approximately $100.
- Estimated net: $800-2,500 depending on fill rate and pricing.

Stronger fill-rate target:

- 7-8 advertisers at blended $650.
- Revenue: $4,550-5,200.
- Costs: approximately $1,700-2,100.
- Estimated net: $2,450-3,500.

## 5. Sales process

### 5.1 Category locking

Build a prospect list across 8-10 categories before outreach. Mark a category as
"locked" only after a signed agreement and deposit are received. Until then, keep
backup prospects active.

Priority advertiser categories:

- HVAC;
- pest control;
- general dentistry;
- pediatric dentistry or orthodontics;
- pool service;
- landscaping;
- plumbing;
- roofing;
- house cleaning; and
- med spa or aesthetics.

### 5.2 Outreach cadence

1. Cold email with community-specific hook.
2. Day-3 follow-up emphasizing category exclusivity.
3. Day-7 value-add email with break-even math.
4. Phone follow-up to the highest-fit businesses.
5. In-person visit for premium local prospects.

### 5.3 Renewal strategy

Follow up two weeks after delivery. Ask what calls, website visits, QR scans, or
coupon redemptions came in. Offer a 15-20% discount for a two- or three-campaign
commitment when paid on the normal 50/50 schedule.

## 6. 30-day launch checklist

### Week 1: legal and market setup

- Choose operating name and county.
- File FBN if needed.
- Confirm local business license requirements.
- Review CDTFA guidance for printed materials and seller's permit obligations.
- Pick the first community, ideally Danville 94526.
- Use EDDM to identify 2-3 viable carrier routes.

### Week 2: sales asset preparation

- Finalize one-page sell sheet or deck.
- Prepare the three-email sequence.
- Build a prospect tracker with 30-40 local businesses.
- Confirm category pricing and premium-placement inventory.
- Prepare the service agreement and creative brief.

### Week 3: outreach

- Send email 1 to top prospects.
- Call the strongest 10 prospects.
- Send email 2 to non-responders.
- Record objections and refine category scripts.
- Lock first advertisers with signed agreements and deposits.

### Week 4: production readiness

- Collect advertiser logos, offers, URLs, phone numbers, and proof requirements.
- Draft postcard layout.
- Get written proof approvals.
- Collect remaining balances.
- Confirm print vendor, bundling requirements, DDU location, and drop date.

## 7. Key risks and controls

| Risk | Control |
| --- | --- |
| Too few advertisers close | Start with lower print volume and 5-6 advertisers; keep backup prospects per category |
| Advertiser expects guaranteed leads | Agreement states no guaranteed response or sales results |
| California tax treatment is unclear | Confirm CDTFA/seller's permit requirements before collecting payments |
| EDDM routes do not match target households | Screenshot and save selected route details before selling the campaign |
| Proof delays push print date | Agreement includes proof deadline and default approval process |
| Competing advertiser dispute | Maintain category-locking notes and define each advertiser's category in writing |
