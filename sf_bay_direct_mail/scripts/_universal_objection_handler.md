# Universal Objection Handler

Use this with any category-specific pitch script. Language is market-neutral
— works as-is for any Bay Area community.

---

## "We don't do direct mail."

> "Totally fair — most service businesses I talk to either tried it 5+ years
> ago or never tried it at all. The reason I lead with the postcard format is
> exactly because it's *not* what your competitors are doing right now.
>
> Everyone's pouring money into Google ads where the cost per click in
> {{Category}} is $25–$60 in the Bay Area. A $650 postcard slot puts your
> brand on the fridge of 6,000 households for 6–8 weeks for about 11 cents a
> home. Different math.
>
> If you can give me 15 minutes and you still don't think it's worth it after
> seeing the numbers, I'll buy you coffee. Fair?"

---

## "We tried it before. Didn't work."

> "What did you mail and where did you mail it?"
>
> *(Listen. 90% of the time it was a flyer to the wrong list, or a postcard
> with no offer, or a single drop with no follow-on.)*
>
> "Yeah — that's almost always why direct mail flops. Three things we do
> differently:
>
> 1. Shared cost on a single high-quality postcard — your $650 buys you 6,000
>    targeted impressions, not 600.
> 2. We filter the EDDM routes by income and household size before we mail.
> 3. We give you a tracking phone number so you actually see what came in.
>
> If we run one campaign and you don't break even, you don't have to renew."

---

## "Too expensive."

> "Compared to what?
>
> Google Ads in {{Category}} runs $25–$60 per click in the Bay Area, and most
> of those clicks bounce. $650 puts you in front of 6,000 owner-occupied
> homes — that's roughly 11 cents per household.
>
> All you need is **3 booked jobs** to break even at your average ticket
> size. Everything after that is margin, on a postcard that lives on the
> fridge for 6–8 weeks."

---

## "Need to think about it."

> "Of course. What specifically do you want to think through?
>
> *(Listen — usually it's spousal/partner approval, cash flow timing, or
> 'I'm not sure my offer is dialed.' Solve the actual blocker.)*
>
> Two things to know on timing: I'm holding the {{Category}} slot for you
> right now, but I have one other lead in the category I'd talk to next. If
> you can give me a yes/no by {{Lock_Deadline}}, I'll keep the slot warm.
> Otherwise it goes to whoever moves first."

---

## "Send me an email and I'll think about it."

> "Happy to. So I send the right thing — are you closer to *yes if the
> numbers make sense* or *no, just curious*? That tells me whether to send
> the deck or just the route map."

---

## "I'm too busy right now."

> "Got it. Quick reframe: this is a passive lead source. Once we lock the
> creative, you don't touch it again until calls come in 2–3 weeks after
> the drop. I do the design, the printing, the EDDM paperwork, and the
> drop-off. Your time commitment is one 30-minute creative meeting and
> approving the proof.
>
> Want me to send a calendar link for the 30-minute creative meeting?"
